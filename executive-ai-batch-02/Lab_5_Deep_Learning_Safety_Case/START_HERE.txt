WHAT THIS LAB IS ABOUT
  A supplier wants to sell your depot an "APU Health Monitor" built on a deep neural
  network. Their pitch: it needs no failure examples to train, it learns what a healthy
  compressor looks like, and it flags anything abnormal -- including fault types you
  have never seen before.

  You are not the person building it. You are the person who has to decide whether it
  can be trusted near a railway. So you will build it yourself, measure it honestly,
  and then write the argument.

  The data is the SAME FILE as Lab 2. That is on purpose -- you already know what the
  sensors mean, so today's thinking goes on the model and the decision instead.

  Fair warning: the evidence does not point cleanly one way. Do not go looking for the
  answer the lab "wants". There isn't one -- there is an argument, and it is yours to make.

THIS WILL FEEL ODD, AND IT IS DELIBERATE
  Most of this lab is spent finding reasons NOT to trust the model you have just built.
  That is not cynicism and it is not a trick question -- it is the job. Anyone can build
  a model. The harder and more valuable skill is being able to say, on the record and
  with numbers, what a model has NOT been shown to do.

  Expect at least one moment where a result you were pleased with stops meaning what
  you thought it meant. When that happens, you are doing the lab correctly.

HOW THE NOTEBOOK WORKS
  Each step is worked and explained, then you DO a "Your turn" cell yourself (nine in
  total). It ends with a two-part "Challenge" -- and the second part has no code in it
  at all. Only one new idea is introduced at a time.

  Your numbers should match the notebook's exactly, because the random seeds are fixed.
  If they do not, check you have not changed a seed or run cells out of order.

THE MAIN DELIVERABLE IS A SAFETY CASE
  After the notebook, fill in Safety_Case_Lab5.docx IN YOUR OWN WORDS. A safety case is
  not a summary of what you did -- it is an ARGUMENT, with a claim, the evidence for it,
  the evidence against it, what being wrong costs, the risk that remains, and a
  recommendation you would put your name to.

  Write it for someone who understands trains, budgets and risk, and who has never
  heard of a neural network.

  Do NOT paste code or notebook outputs into it. Quote a number as evidence where it
  helps your argument. A safety case that is just pasted output scores no marks -- and
  in the real world it earns no signature.
